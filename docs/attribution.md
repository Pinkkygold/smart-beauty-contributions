# Attribution and evidence boundaries

## Evidence levels

- **Locally verified:** existing company web repository history attributes a merged seller verification contribution and two CI-related changes to my Git identity.
- **Self-reported:** broader AI responsibilities, historical QA counts, translation coverage results, RTL checks, and Figma implementation experience come from my internship account.
- **Team context:** the broader Smart Beauty product, marketplace, payment infrastructure, mobile application, and design system are team/company work.

Local verification supports contribution attribution. It does not grant permission to disclose proprietary material or establish legal ownership of work created during employment.

## Attribution rules

I do not claim to have created Smart Beauty from scratch, implemented the whole marketplace or backend, designed every screen, developed every AI capability, or authored another developer's case-study repository.

Figma descriptions cover my interpretation and implementation process. Claims of original design authorship require separate supporting evidence.

## Repository contents

This repository is newly written documentation. No files have been copied from the company application into it, and it does not include company Git history. Company commit identifiers, private repository links, and internal implementation paths are intentionally not needed to understand the case study.

The raw internship context is not included: it contains internal details that are unnecessary for a portfolio. The documents retain contribution-level descriptions and clearly qualify historical validation figures.

## Review before public sharing

Keep the repository private while reviewing whether the company name, project name, contribution descriptions, and historical figures may be publicly shared under applicable agreements. Privacy reduces exposure but is not a substitute for permission where required.
